02 / THE SILENT PROBLEM

≈589 million
live with diabetes.

ADULTS AGED 20–79 · IDF DIABETES ATLAS, 11TH EDITION, 2025 ↗

High blood sugar wears down the longest nerves first. The toes go quiet, then the ball of the foot, then the rest.

Without feeling, pain stops doing its job. A stone in a shoe or a blister goes unnoticed for days, and a small wound can turn into an ulcer.

03 / THE TEST

Ten grams.
Give or take.

The standard screen is a nylon monofilament. A clinician presses it against the sole until it bends, at about 10 grams of force. If the patient can’t feel it, that part of the foot has lost its protective sensation.

  2. 02 Load rises
  3. 03 Buckles at ≈ 10 g
  4. 04 But which 10 g?

1bit

Is all it reports

A filament says one thing: it bent. Not how hard, not for how long, and nothing goes on record.

  1. 1896

    Max von Frey measures touch with calibrated hairs, each bending at its own force.

  2. 1960

    Josephine Semmes and Sidney Weinstein switch to nylon filaments. The filaments still carry their names.

  3. 1986

    At the leprosy hospital in Carville, Louisiana, missing the 10 g filament is linked to foot-ulcer risk.

  4. Today

    Guidelines from the IWGDF call for checking protective sensation in every person with diabetes, at least once a year.

SoleSense keeps the simple test
and adds the missing number.

04 / INSIDE · 1 OF 5

One handle.
Two heads.

A pressure head and a vibration head share one handle. Each locks on with a 30° twist and a safety slider, and a four-wire harness runs through the joint.

  • PRESSURE HEAD
  • VIBRATION HEAD
  • 30° TWIST-LOCK
04 / INSIDE · 2 OF 5

Lift the lid.
Three parts do the work.

Inside the 76 × 56 × 44 mm pressure head sit a printed spring, a pair of magnets and a Hall sensor. Four long screws from underneath hold the cover on.

  • 76 × 56 × 44 MM
  • 4 × M3 × 35
  • REPLACEABLE CARTRIDGE
04 / INSIDE · 3 OF 5

A spring
you can print.

The teal cassette is one flat print: a fixed rail, two thin beams and a moving rail. Nothing slides in the sensing path, so nothing rubs. Swap in 1.0 or 1.2 mm beams for a stiffer spring.

  • ONE PART
  • 2 BEAMS, 27 MM APART
  • 0.8 · 1.0 · 1.2 MM
04 / INSIDE · 4 OF 5

A magnet that moves.
A sensor that stays.

Two Ø3 mm disc magnets ride on the blue carriage with the filament. Five millimetres away, a fixed 49E Hall sensor reads their field: about 17 mT at rest, about 50 mT at the 2 mm travel stop.

  • 5.0 MM GAP
  • ≈ 17 → 50 MT
  • 2.0 MM STOP
04 / INSIDE · 5 OF 5

The brains
in the handle.

An ESP32 reads the sensor. A 0.96 in screen shows the reading, a microSD card logs every sample, and an 18650 cell with USB-C charging powers the lot.

  • ESP32
  • 0.96 IN OLED
  • MICROSD LOG
  • 18650 · USB-C
05 / PRESS IT

Press
and hold.

The tip pushes the carriage, the beams bend, the magnet closes on the sensor and the reading climbs. Try a stiffer spring and feel the difference.
